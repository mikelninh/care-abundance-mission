# Mission Architecture

## The problem

A government can pass a law, allocate money, announce a programme and still fail the person it intended to help.

CARE treats delivery as an end-to-end system:

`right → eligibility → calculation → application → decision → payment/service → receipt → reconciliation → appeal`

A promise is not considered delivered until the intended person can verify that the money or resource actually arrived.

## The abundance thesis

CARE is not only a welfare-distribution project. The mission has two simultaneous objectives:

1. **Bake more cake:** increase Germany's real productive capacity.
2. **Make sure people receive the upside:** improve wages, services, affordability, public wealth and direct household resources.

The useful household-level concept is broader than GDP:

`Household Abundance = disposable income + transfers + public-service value - essential costs`

The national system therefore keeps two scoreboards:

### Productive capacity

- real GDP per capita;
- output per hour;
- business formation and investment;
- AI/automation adoption;
- energy cost and reliability;
- housing completion and cost;
- infrastructure performance;
- R&D diffusion;
- exports and trade intensity.

### Human delivery

- median real disposable household resources;
- housing/energy/food/transport burden;
- legal entitlement discovered;
- entitlement received;
- delivery gap;
- processing time;
- appeal/correction rate;
- public wealth per resident;
- any citizen prosperity dividend.

## Core CARE metrics

For person or household `i`:

`DeliveryGap_i = max(Entitlement_i - Delivered_i, 0)`

At system level:

`CareDeliveryRate = DeliveredResources / LegallyOwedResources`

The implementation should also track service delivery where money is the wrong unit: appointments, childcare places, housing, education, mobility, health services and other public resources.

## Policy Proof

Political proposals should become executable, auditable scenarios rather than slogans.

For a fixed household CARE should show:

- current-law monthly resources;
- proposal A/B/C monthly resources;
- change in euros;
- duration/guarantee period;
- gross fiscal cost;
- funding sources;
- behavioural assumptions;
- uncertainty range;
- source text and legal implementation required.

No funding proof should mean `UNFUNDED` or `PARTIALLY FUNDED`, not a partisan judgement.

## Growth Engine

Each growth intervention should carry:

- baseline;
- intervention;
- implementation owner;
- public/private investment required;
- expected mechanism;
- modelled effect range;
- time horizon;
- distributional effect;
- precedent country or programme;
- source;
- confidence level;
- realised outcome after launch.

Effects must not be naively added. AI, capital investment, energy, labour and administrative reforms interact and overlap.

## Initial high-leverage domains

1. AI and automation diffusion.
2. Once-only digital government and lower administrative burden.
3. Work incentives where extra work always increases net household resources.
4. Outcome-driven use of the €500bn infrastructure/climate-neutrality fund.
5. Abundant reliable energy.
6. Housing and construction throughput.
7. Skills, childcare and labour participation.
8. Entrepreneurship, capital formation and R&D diffusion.
9. International trade and growth corridors.
10. Citizen ownership of part of publicly enabled upside.

## Germany–Vietnam Growth Corridor

Vietnam is the first corridor because substantial infrastructure already exists: bilateral trade, German firms in Vietnam, German exporters, a large Vietnamese diaspora in Germany, and the EU–Vietnam trade agreement.

The corridor should map:

`need → capability → company → trade rule → certification → financing → introduction → contract → jobs/investment → verified outcome`

Priority sectors include industrial AI, manufacturing, energy, health/medtech, vocational education, semiconductors, software and climate infrastructure.

The long-term goal is to replicate the corridor logic with other complementary economies.

## Citizen ownership

Growing GDP is not enough if households cannot feel the gain.

Policy experiments should therefore model mechanisms that can connect publicly enabled growth to citizens, including:

- higher wages and labour income;
- lower essential costs;
- better universal services;
- higher public revenue used transparently;
- public investment returns;
- citizen-owned prosperity funds;
- equity/revenue participation where government materially de-risks private upside;
- direct dividends where legally and fiscally justified.

The precise mechanism is a political choice. CARE's job is to make the mechanism and arithmetic explicit.

## Agent architecture

The mission can be supported continuously by agents, but external actions remain permissioned.

`Evidence Watch + Policy Watch + Corridor Watch → Evidence Store → Judge/Verifier → Mission Control → Human Approval → External Action`

Agents may automatically:

- monitor sources;
- detect stale facts;
- identify opportunities;
- run calculations;
- prioritise experiments;
- prepare drafts and briefs;
- flag unsupported claims.

Agents should not autonomously:

- move money;
- submit legally binding applications;
- contact officials or companies as the user without approval;
- publish accusations;
- overwrite evidence history.

## North star

The aim is not a bigger state for its own sake, nor a smaller state for its own sake.

The aim is a state and economy that measurably expand human possibility — and can prove what actually reached people.
